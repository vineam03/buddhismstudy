These texts were composed in a world of kings, castes, and rice paddies — but they were composed about craving, status, love, anger, and death, which have not shipped a new version since. Each lens takes one region of contemporary life and shows where the canon already mapped it: the situation, the mechanism, and a concrete playbook. Every claim links to its source card in the library, so nothing here floats free of the texts.

1. Career & Work

Ambition, reviews, burnout, bad managers, layoffs, and what a job is for

The promotion treadmill

The mechanism is the canon's opening case study: acquire, plateau, recalibrate, crave again. MN 26 calls chasing durable security through perishable positions "the ignoble search" and makes a precise prediction: the anxiety attached to it is structural — it survives every promotion, because each acquisition resets the baseline rather than closing the gap. Anyone who has landed the long-craved title and felt the wanting simply reload has run the experiment. This doesn't argue against ambition; it argues against expecting ambition's payoff to be peace. Work can deliver mastery, contribution, and honest income (AN 4.62 endorses all three); it cannot deliver the thing craving is actually reaching for, and knowing the difference is what keeps a career from becoming a hamster wheel with a retirement plan.

Performance reviews and feedback

The eight worldly winds — gain/loss, status/disgrace, praise/blame, pleasure/pain — now arrive as dashboards, ratings, and calibration meetings, but they rotate exactly as scheduled. The untrained response is the one the sutta describes: engrossed by the good quarter, capsized by the bad one — treating metrics as verdicts on the self rather than weather passing through a career. DN 1 opens with the same training: the Buddha catches monks elated by praise and deflated by blame of him, and points out that both reactions cloud judgment — you can't evaluate feedback accurately while it's evaluating you. The practical stance: read the review as data about the work, refuse it as a referendum on the person, and notice that everyone senior enough to write your review is riding the same eight winds.

Burnout and the effort dial

AN 6.55 is the definitive text: Soṇa practiced until his feet bled, then swung to wanting to quit entirely — and the Buddha's diagnosis was that overexertion doesn't step down to sustainability; it snaps to abandonment. That oscillation — sprint, crash, quit, guilt, sprint — is the standard modern burnout loop, and the lute-string answer is calibration, not intensity: too tight snaps, too loose won't play, and the skill is checking your own pitch regularly instead of discovering the snapped string at the exit interview. MN 36 supplies the founder-level evidence that punishment-driven effort fails at scale: six years of world-class austerity bought nothing, and the breakthrough came from the question grind culture never asks — "why am I afraid of wholesome pleasure?" Rest, play, and warmth are not what serious people give up; they're what sustainable seriousness runs on.

Managing and being managed

The canon has an actual management section: DN 31 assigns employers duties that sound like a modern handbook — assign work matched to ability, pay fairly, provide care in sickness, share unusual gains, give time off — and assigns workers the reciprocals. DN 26 scales it up: the mythical kingdom collapses when the king punishes theft without addressing the poverty that caused it — the founding text on why performance-managing a deprived team accelerates the spiral. Check what's been withheld (resources, clarity, fairness) before reaching for discipline. And SN 47.19 is the manager's oxygen-mask: two acrobats each responsible for their own balance make a stable act — your own regulation is the load-bearing contribution to everyone standing on the pole with you, and a dysregulated leader "supporting" a team is the apprentice trying to hold the master's pole.

Layoffs and job loss

A layoff is the aggregates teaching (SN 22.59) delivered without anesthetic: the role, badge, calendar, and title were configurations you ran, not the self that ran them — which is why their loss can feel like death to someone who thought the title was the person, and like a brutal reconfiguration to someone who didn't. The chariot argument is the repair manual: parts can be re-arranged; essences can only be mourned. AN 3.99 adds the trajectory: the same salt-lump of misfortune ruins a cup and doesn't register in a river — the months after a job loss are precisely when expanding the container (skills, relationships, health, practice) changes what the event means in the story of the life. And the two-arrow discipline (SN 36.6) matters most here: the layoff is the first arrow; the "I'm finished, I always fail, everyone knows" cascade is a second arrow with your own fletching on it.

Deep work in a shallow environment

What the canon calls guarding the sense doors (DN 2, SN 35.28) is what productivity literature reinvented as attention management: input governs cognition, and an ungoverned input stream produces an ungoverned mind. The Vinaya's structure — fixed hours, restrained senses, single-tasked ritual — is a deep-work protocol with robes on. The working translation: your feeds, tabs, and notifications are sense-doors; MN 19's law ("whatever one frequently thinks upon becomes the inclination of the mind") means every interruption you accept is a rep for interruptibility itself.

Playbook

  • Before accepting a role or project, run the two-searches sort (MN 26): what part of this is mastery and contribution, what part is craving's next baseline-reset?
  • Weekly lute-check (AN 6.55): restless and fried, or dull and drifting? Retune by small turns, not dramatic gestures.
  • Reviews: extract the data, decline the verdict (AN 8.6).
  • Managing: provision before punishment (DN 26); your own stability first (SN 47.19).
  • One guarded sense-door per workday: a two-hour block where nothing rings, pings, or scrolls.

2. Love & Romantic Relationships

Attachment, the recurring fight, temptation, breakups, and the price on the tag

The contract nobody reads

MN 87 states the terms without softening: sorrow is born from the beloved — loving mortal beings is exposure to grief, and no relationship strategy negotiates the clause away. The sutta's genius is the demonstration: Queen Mallikā doesn't argue the point to her offended husband; she asks it — "Do you love your daughter? Would her change grieve you?" — until the king feels the contract he'd already signed. This is not an argument against love. It's the honest price tag, and reading it changes behavior in one specific direction: from background dread to present-tense attention. The people you'll someday grieve are alive right now, and every hour of irritated coexistence is spent from the same account as the hours you'll wish back.

Love versus its counterfeit

The canon's word for trained love — mettā (Snp 1.8) — means wanting the other's welfare. Its near-enemy, the counterfeit that fools everyone, is attachment: wanting the other's presence, approval, and services, which is craving wearing love's clothes. The two feel identical in good weather and opposite in bad: goodwill survives the partner changing, needing less of you, or choosing what you wouldn't; attachment experiences all three as betrayal. The diagnostic question — "am I loving this person or consuming them?" — is uncomfortable precisely as often as it's useful. SN 3.8 adds the foundation stone: everyone is dearest to themselves, your partner included, and love built on that honest symmetry (two centers of two worlds, negotiating) outlasts love built on the fantasy of merger.

The fight you keep having

Recurring couple-fights run on papañca (MN 18): a two-second tone of voice → unpleasant feeling → evaluation → and within three minutes the internal prosecutor has indicted the entire relationship, with exhibits from 2019. The sutta's reversal explains the escalation: what you proliferate turns around and assails you — the story you built from their sigh is now attacking you, and you respond to your story, not their sigh. MN 21's maid-test supplies the humbling frame: your gentleness rating, like Lady Vedehikā's, is only measured under provocation — and your partner, unlike your colleagues, holds the complete provocation map. The repair sequence is MN 48's, and the order matters: small acts of goodwill first, litigation of the issue second — arguing the issue on a depleted goodwill account is the losing order of operations every couples therapist rediscovers.

Temptation and fidelity

The Journey West circle-episode is the fidelity teaching in one image: the protection only works from inside, it is exited voluntarily, usually with sympathetic-sounding reasons, at appetite's urging — and the demon never once looks like a demon. The Vinaya's 227 case studies agree: nobody's downfall story starts with "I decided to ruin my life"; it starts with a plausible kindness, a special circumstance, an exception. MN 2 dignifies avoidance as a legitimate tool, not cowardice: some situations are not tests to pass but doors not to open, and knowing which category you're in before the situation is what the reflection is for.

Breakups and divorce

A breakup is compound loss: the person, the imagined future, and the self-configuration that existed only in that relationship — which is why it triggers identity vertigo beyond the grief. The chariot frame names what actually dissolved (a configuration, not your essence), SN 12.61 warns against trusting the 2 a.m. mind's verdicts about being unlovable (that's a branch-grab, not a judge), and SN 36.6 separates the honest first arrow (real loss, real pain) from the optional volley — the rumination reel, the case-building, the surveillance of their new life. AN 5.161's fifth rung is built for exes: their conduct is their own ledger, and resigning from the volunteer-judge position is not forgiveness theater — it's putting down unpaid work.

Playbook

  • Run Mallikā's questions (MN 87) quarterly; convert the answer into one act of present appreciation per day.
  • In conflict: name the papañca handoff — "I'm responding to my story now, not to you" (MN 18).
  • Repair order: goodwill acts first, issue second (MN 48).
  • Know your circle-exits in advance (MN 2): the situations you don't walk into beat the temptations you out-argue.
  • Post-breakup: no verdicts from the midnight mind (SN 12.61); second-arrow audit daily (SN 36.6).

3. Family & Parenting

Raising children, aging parents, inherited patterns, and the six directions at home

Teaching children

The canon's parenting pedagogy is MN 61: the Buddha teaches his own seven-year-old with the props at hand — a water dipper, an elephant story — and installs exactly two things: truthfulness as the foundation (the shameless liar has "thrown away" the mechanism every other virtue depends on) and the mirror practice (check the act before, during, and after: does it harm me, others, both?). Note what's absent: doctrine, threats, and shame. When review finds harm done, the prescribed response is disclosure and course-correction — confession culture, not punishment culture — which is also the Vinaya's entire disciplinary architecture: name the act, restore the standard, move on. A household that runs on "tell me the truth and we'll fix it" is running MN 61; a household that runs on "don't get caught" is training exactly what it fears.

The teenager and the element meditations

MN 62 is literally a curriculum for an adolescent — Rāhula as a teenager, stung by correction, gets the element meditations: be like earth, which receives filth and flowers without flinching; like water, washing foul and clean alike; like space, established nowhere. For the parent, the same meditations are survival equipment: adolescence throws filth and flowers in rapid alternation, and the parent who can be earth — receiving both without capsizing — is the stable surface a teenager secretly requires while loudly rejecting. The antidote-pairing in the same sutta (goodwill against ill-will, equanimity against irritation) is the evening reset after the door slams.

Aging parents

DN 31 puts parents in the east — the dawn direction, first honored — with concrete duties: support them as they supported you, keep the family's good name, be worthy of the inheritance (the character, not just the assets). The five recollections do the emotional preparation the culture refuses: "they are subject to aging, they have not gone beyond it" — said daily, it converts the shock of a parent's decline into something witnessed rather than denied, and it reorders priorities while reordering still helps. DN 16 models the endgame: the Buddha's last months — old, sick, "an old cart held together with straps" — were spent resolving questions and releasing guilt in the people around him. Helping a parent finish clean (questions asked, recordings made, forgiveness exchanged, logistics settled) is the last duty of the eastern direction, and the one most postponed until it can't be done.

Inherited patterns

Every family transmits kamma in the ordinary sense: the anger style, the money fear, the silence protocol — each generation inheriting the previous one's damage and normalizing it, which is DN 26's decline mechanism running at household scale. The hopeful clause is the same sutta's floor: the spiral reverses when one person keeps one standard — "let us at least stop [the yelling / the secrets / the cutting remarks]" — and holds it long enough to become the new normal someone else inherits. AN 3.99 is the recovering family's charter: the inherited salt cannot be un-dropped, but the container can be enlarged until the old pattern stops flavoring everything. And MN 135 keeps the ledger honest in both directions: you are heir to your parents' actions and owner of your own — explanation is not exemption.

Playbook

  • Install the mirror, not the fear (MN 61): "did it harm you, them, both — and what do we fix?"
  • Parent-of-teenager daily: one element meditation (MN 62), chosen to match the day's weather.
  • Say the recollections about your parents specifically (AN 5.57) — then make the visit or the call.
  • Name your family's inherited pattern out loud once (DN 26); pick the one standard you'll hold against it.

4. Friendship & Community

Choosing company, keeping it, repairing it, and the loneliness question

The strongest claim in the canon

When Ānanda proposed that good friendship is half the holy life, the Buddha corrected him upward: it is the whole of it (SN 45.2). Not a warm sentiment — a causal claim: transformation is socially transmitted, and who you practice beside predicts your development better than technique or talent, both of which flow through relationships anyway. Modern behavioral research keeps re-deriving this (habits, moods, even health metrics propagate through social graphs); the canon simply built its entire institution — the Sangha — on the finding, twenty-four centuries before the data.

Auditing the roster

DN 31 provides the field guide: four fake friends (the taker, the big talker, the flatterer, the partner-in-ruin) and four true ones (the helper, the constant in good times and bad, the honest counselor, the one who feels for you). The audit is meant kindly but cuts: the flatterer is the hardest to convict because the evidence feels so good, and the partner-in-ruin is usually the most fun person you know. The modern extension is non-optional: feeds, podcasts, and group chats are companions now — MN 19's law doesn't distinguish between a friend who rehearses grievance with you and an algorithm that does. Deliberate company-keeping — seeking out one person whose character you want to catch — is the highest-leverage practice in this entire section, per Khp 5, which puts "avoiding fools, keeping wise company" at the very bottom of the blessing-ladder: the prerequisite everything else stands on.

When the group fractures

Kosambī (MN 48) is the case study for friend-group schisms, team splits, and community blowups alike: the stated issue is trivial (there, bathroom etiquette), because the real failure was upstream — cordiality maintenance stopped, and the backlog made any spark sufficient. The six principles are infrastructure, not niceness: acts of goodwill in body, speech, and thought (public and private — the private half is where contempt incubates), sharing gains, common standards. The repair order bears repeating because every instinct fights it: restore the fabric first, litigate the tear second. And the historical coda is comfort for anyone who's watched adults behave badly: even the Buddha, mid-quarrel, couldn't argue that community down — he left and lived with an elephant until the laity's displeasure did what reasoning couldn't.

Loneliness and chosen solitude

The canon holds both poles honestly. The rhinoceros horn praises solitude — much of what passes for connection is mutual dependency, and the capacity to be alone is the prerequisite for company that isn't need in a costume. But the same poem pauses to say: find a wise companion and walk with them gladly; wander alone for lack of such, not as a lifestyle brand. The modern epidemic is usually neither pole but the worst hybrid — physically alone while parasocially crowded, solitude's costs without its benefits. The practical sort: schedule true solitude (no input) until it stops being frightening, and spend the sociability budget on the true-friend categories rather than on ambient contact.

Playbook

  • List your five highest-hour companions, feeds included; label each path-support or path-drag (SN 45.2).
  • Run the fake/true friend audit without cynicism — then be auditable: which category are you in your friends' lists? (DN 31)
  • Group strain: six-cordialities check — when did the private goodwill stop? (MN 48)
  • One input-free walk per week (Snp 1.3): what surfaces is what the noise was managing.

5. Money, Wealth & Security

Debt, budgets, the craving loop, generosity, and what money can't hold

The four accounts

The canon is not anti-money — its personal-finance teachings went to Anāthapiṇḍika, a financier (AN 4.62), and they endorse earning and enjoying without embarrassment. But they rank the accounts: ownership, enjoyment, and debtlessness together are worth a sixteenth of blamelessness. That exchange rate is the section's master principle: no portfolio compensates for things to hide, and the felt quality of a financial life is dominated by conscience — the clean-money premium is real and paid in sleep. DN 2's first "fruit" makes the same point from the other side: the happiness of the debtless man is that he is unafraid. Debt is fear with a payment schedule, and the canon treats eliminating it as a happiness intervention, not just an accounting one.

The reservoir

AN 8.54 models a household economy as a reservoir: inflow (skill and diligence at the actual trade), containment (guarding what's earned), and outflow — four classic drains (intoxication, gambling, affairs, bad company) that empty a reservoir with the inlets wide open. The modern audit translates directly: most people optimize the inlet (income, side hustles) while ignoring open channels — the subscription bleed, the vice budget, the expensive social circle, the convenience premium on every transaction. DN 31 adds the allocation rule (enjoy a quarter, invest half, save a quarter) and the drain list's ancestor: six ways wealth leaks, with "sleeping late" and "bad company" ranked alongside gambling. Balanced living bans both failure modes at once — beyond-your-means and the joyless hoard.

The craving loop, monetized

Consumer culture is dependent origination with a checkout flow: contact (the ad) → feeling (lack, engineered) → craving → clinging (cart) → becoming (the person who owns it) → and the quiet aftermath where the baseline resets and the loop re-arms (SN 12.2). Retail therapy is the second arrow monetized — numbing purchased because no other pain-exit was ever taught — and advertising is professional papañca-engineering (MN 18): its entire craft is installing the story in which the product is the missing piece of you. The countermeasure is the pause at the feeling-link: the engineered lack is real as a feeling and false as a fact, and it dissolves on a 72-hour delay more often than not.

Security and the thing money can't hold

Under all of it runs the wager that enough money finally equals safety — the ignoble search again: perishable stores can be enjoyed but not leaned on, and the leaning is what generates the anxiety that more acquisition claims to cure. The fifth recollection names the only asset that actually transfers: "I am the owner of my actions" — everything else is, in the strict sense, on loan. Generosity is the canon's hedge against money's psychological failure modes: Iti 27's giving-teaching ("if beings knew the fruit of giving as I do, they would not eat without sharing") treats it not as charity but as training — the direct counter-rep to the contraction that wealth otherwise installs.

Playbook

  • Fund the four accounts in order — and audit the blamelessness account quarterly (AN 4.62).
  • Map your reservoir: name your top two open channels this month (AN 8.54).
  • 72-hour rule on unplanned purchases: let the engineered lack expire (SN 12.2).
  • Automate one recurring act of generosity at a level you don't have to re-decide (Iti 27).
  • Debt paydown reframed as fear reduction, tracked in sleep quality, not just interest saved (DN 2).

6. Digital Life & Attention

Feeds, outrage, notifications, parasocial life, and the six sense-doors online

The feed as sense-door

The canon's most modern-sounding teaching is its oldest frame: experience arrives through six doors, and what enters shapes what you become (SN 35.28, DN 2). A feed is a sense-door with a mind on the other side optimizing for your arousal — the first sense-door in history that studies you back. "All is burning" was preached to fire-worshippers about their own senses; the engagement economy has simply built better bellows. The design target of infinite scroll is precisely the unguarded door, and MN 19 prices the exposure: every session is reps — not of the content's positions, but of its tempo: reactivity, comparison, and the two-second attention hop become the inclination of the mind that practices them nightly.

The outrage economy

The Aṭṭhakavagga — the canon's oldest layer — describes debate culture with eerie precision: adopting positions as identity, the surge when your side scores, the dejection when it loses, both wounds. The quote-tweet economy is that tournament with distribution: platforms profit per engagement, and nothing engages like identity-threat, so the feed is structurally biased toward showing you the elephant-patch most likely to enrage you (Ud 6.4 — everyone touching real elephant, everyone certain, algorithmically prevented from comparing notes). AN 7.64 completes the accounting: the anger you carry out of the app — sleep, judgment, face — is paid from your account, and the provocation's author collects nothing but engagement metrics. You are not the customer of your own outrage.

Doomscrolling and the second arrow

Compulsive scrolling is SN 36.6's diagnosis running on a battery: struck by the first arrow (boredom, anxiety, loneliness), the untrained person "seeks escape in sensual distraction — because he knows no other exit." The scroll is the modern default exit, and it has the anesthetic's exact profile: works instantly, wears off faster each time, and leaves the wound untreated plus a dosage problem. The sutta's alternative isn't abstinence heroics; it's learning an actual exit — feeling the first arrow fully (three breaths of "this is boredom, and it's survivable") before the hand moves. Half the reaching stops when the feeling underneath gets thirty seconds of direct attention.

Parasocial company and the comparison engine

SN 45.2's claim — you become your company — has no exemption clause for company that doesn't know you exist. Hours with a streamer, a podcast host, or a curated feed of strangers' peak moments are formation hours. The comparison engine runs on a perceptual error the canon names precisely: you compare your inside (the full aggregate feed, doubts included) against others' outsides (a highlight reel — their form aggregate, professionally lit). Muditā practice (SN 46.54) is the direct counter-training — deliberately wishing a rival's visible success larger — and the recollections restore the missing data: every account you envy ages, sickens, loses, and owns its actions, exactly as you do.

A digital uposatha

The tradition's own rhythm suggests the fix: the uposatha — the fortnightly day of intensified restraint the laity kept alongside the monastics — is a technology sabbath with 2,400 years of user testing. Not permanent renunciation: a recurring, scheduled, communal restraint window, which succeeds where willpower fails because it's a calendar fact rather than a nightly renegotiation (MN 2's avoidance-tool, institutionalized). One screen-free evening a week, kept like an appointment, outperforms a hundred resolutions to "use the phone less."

Playbook

  • Before opening the app, name the first arrow: what feeling is this reach exiting? (SN 36.6)
  • One tournament-free week per month: hold your views, enter no contests (Snp 4).
  • Audit parasocial hours as company hours (SN 45.2): would you hire these formators?
  • Muditā rep on the comparison spike: wish that specific success larger, and watch what it does to the envy (SN 46.54).
  • Standing digital uposatha: same evening weekly, no exceptions to renegotiate.

7. Conflict, Anger & Communication

Hard conversations, criticism in both directions, grudges, and apology

Before the hard conversation

MN 58's three gates — true, beneficial, timely — with "welcome" deliberately excluded: some truths must wound (the stick in the choking child's mouth comes out even if it draws blood — silence, there, is the unkindness), and many true things fail the useful-or-timely gates and should die unsaid. Most delivered criticism passes gate one only: true, but aimed at self-expression rather than benefit, timed for the speaker's relief rather than the listener's reception. AN 5.198 adds the two carriers — gentle wording, and the inner state underneath: the same sentence from goodwill and from irritation are different communications, and every listener's nervous system can tell before the content arrives.

Receiving criticism

The canon trains the receiving side just as hard. DN 1 opens the entire collection with it: don't be deflated by blame or elated by praise — both reactions cloud the only useful question, which is what part of this is accurate? The elephant check converts even hostile feedback into data: what part of the animal is this person actually touching? (They're usually touching something.) And MN 21 sets the training ceiling that deletes the negotiating position: if hatred is off the table even for bandits with a saw, the harsh email loses its standing as justification. The working question in the moment is never "is my anger justified?" (often yes, and irrelevant) but "is it doing my work?"

Grudges and their removal

AN 5.161 is a graded protocol, honest about difficulty: goodwill toward the person if you can manage it; compassion ("they're struggling too") if not; mere equanimity if not that; strategic inattention — stop collecting evidence, mute the feed, end the shower-argument rehearsals — if even equanimity fails; and as the floor that always holds, the ownership reflection: their conduct is generating its own consequences without your supervision. Most resentment is unpaid work — a volunteer judgeship on a case the universe is already hearing — and the fifth rung is a resignation letter. AN 7.64 supplies the motivation the moral argument can't: seven costs, all paid by you, delivered to your adversary free; anger is your enemy's unpaid employee, and reviewing your last blowup against the list (check the sleep line first) is usually persuasive where "anger is wrong" never is.

Apology and repair — the Vinaya's secret gift

The Vinaya's deepest export to lay life is its repair architecture. Most offenses are cleared by confession: name the act specifically, to a person, out loud — after which the matter is settled, not archived for later prosecution. No groveling, no permanent record, and no pretending it didn't happen: the standard is restored and the community moves. Compare the two failure modes every family and team knows — the apology-refusal culture where nothing is ever acknowledged, and the shame culture where every acknowledgment becomes ammunition. The confession model threads them: acknowledgment is safe because it's final. The Adhikaraṇasamatha procedures add the group version, including the extraordinary "covering with grass" — when full litigation of every wrong would destroy the community, both sides may confess in bulk and bury the quarrel. Some fights are settled by justice; some, the canon admits, are settled by grass.

Playbook

  • Three gates before sending, in order — and the goodwill check last: rewrite or wait if it fails (MN 58, AN 5.198).
  • Receiving: extract the elephant-part, discard the delivery (Ud 6.4, DN 1).
  • Grudge triage: take the highest rung that actually holds today (AN 5.161).
  • Apologize by confession standard: specific act, out loud, then closed (Vinaya model).
  • Post-blowup: price it against the seven-item receipt (AN 7.64).

8. Health, Body & Energy

Chronic pain, sleep, eating, exercise, body image, and the old cart

Chronic pain and illness

The two arrows were taught for exactly this: pain is the first arrow; the catastrophe-forecast, the "why me," the war against the sensation — those are the second, and in chronic conditions the second arrow often outweighs the first by the end of a day. Modern pain science agrees to a startling degree: resistance and threat-appraisal measurably amplify pain processing. The trained move isn't stoic denial but the sutta's exact phrase — "one feeling, not two": full permission for the sensation, zero subscription to the commentary. DN 16 models the endgame stance: the Buddha, old and sick, describes his body as "an old cart held together with straps" — and keeps teaching. The body's decline is managed like weather-damaged equipment, without the extra suffering of betrayal ("my body is doing this to me" is the second arrow with a possessive pronoun — SN 22.59: if it were truly yours, it would obey).

Sleep

The canon's sleep prescriptions are behavioral and dose-specific. Three of mettā's eleven benefits are literally sleep outcomes — falls asleep easily, wakes easily, no bad dreams — because a mind not rehearsing hostility settles; the goodwill practice is a sleep intervention checkable within weeks. AN 4.62 adds the debtlessness effect ("unafraid" is a sleep descriptor), and DN 2's "blamelessness" fruit is the deepest layer: the background anxiety of self-management — the things to hide, the stories to keep straight — is a nocturnal workload. The canon's insomnia protocol, in effect: clean up one hidden thing, forgive one ledger, and radiate goodwill at the ceiling until the system stands down.

Eating and moderation

Moderation in eating gets its own slot in the gradual training — before meditation, notably: an unregulated relationship with food destabilizes everything downstream. The frame is instrumental, not moral: eat "not for fun, not for intoxication, but for the maintenance of the body… for supporting the holy life" — food as fuel and medicine rather than as mood-management, which quietly diagnoses most modern eating trouble as a second-arrow problem: the snack is anesthesia for a feeling that got no other hearing. Bajie in the Journey West reading is the honest mascot: appetite is never expelled from the pilgrimage — it's given a bounded, useful role, and the goal is conversion, not annihilation.

Exercise and the middle way of discipline

MN 36 is the canon's verdict on punitive training culture, delivered by its most credible witness: six years of maximum-intensity bodily austerity — the ancient world's most extreme fitness regimen, pursued with world-class willpower — produced nothing but damage, and the breakthrough came through nourishment and the un-feared wholesome pleasure of the rose-apple memory. The application writes itself: the sustainable program you keep beats the heroic program you quit (AN 6.55's lute again — and note the failure mode: overtraining doesn't step down, it snaps to the couch). MN 19 supplies the physiology of habit the fitness industry rediscovered: repetition is destiny; the workout's main product is the inclination to work out.

Body image

The Therīgāthā's ex-courtesan poems (see the canon guide) are the tradition's unflinching testimony: women whose livelihood was beauty watching it decay, and finding on the far side of that loss a self-respect no mirror had ever supplied. The aggregate analysis (SN 22.59) names the underlying error — treating the form-aggregate as the self, then experiencing every gray hair as an identity attack — and the first recollection converts the mirror from enemy to teacher: "I am subject to aging" said before the reflection surprises you. The body is an instrument to maintain, not a verdict to appeal.

Playbook

  • Pain flare: narrate the arrows — sensation granted, commentary declined (SN 36.6).
  • Insomnia stack: one confession, one ledger closed, five minutes of ceiling-mettā (AN 11.15).
  • Before eating: fuel or anesthesia? If anesthesia — what's the first arrow? (MN 107)
  • Program design by lute-string: the version you'll still be doing in a year (AN 6.55).

9. Loss, Grief & Mortality

Grieving, accompanying grievers, anticipatory grief, and finishing clean

Inside grief

The canon's grief masterpiece is an errand: Kisāgotamī (Thig 10.1), mad with loss, is not argued with, hurried, or lectured on impermanence — she's sent for a mustard seed from a house death hasn't visited, and the door-to-door search is the cure: her private catastrophe re-scales into the human commons. The movement from "why me" to "so this is what everyone has been carrying" is the door out of grief's isolation, and it opens through contact with other grievers — the grief group is the mustard-seed walk, institutionalized. SN 15.3 offers the same medicine at cosmic dosage: you have already wept an ocean; this pain is the oldest current in sentient life, and you are not uniquely cursed — you are participating in the standard contract. And MN 87 dignifies the pain itself: grief is love's receipt, which is more respectful than any consolation that argues it away.

Accompanying the grieving

Kisāgotamī's healer is the model: presence, gentleness, and a doable task — consolation as errand, not lecture. The standing temptation is to explain (impermanence! meaning! silver linings!), and the canon's own best teacher conspicuously didn't: arguments don't reach grief. What reaches: being one more house that admits its dead — sharing your own losses plainly — and logistics carried without being asked. AN 5.198's "timely" gate does heavy work here: nearly everything true about loss is untimely in the first weeks; the speech that passes all five gates at a funeral is usually short.

Anticipatory grief and the recollections

The five recollections are grief rehearsal with a repurposed payoff: said daily ("all that is mine, beloved and pleasing, will become separated from me"), they don't darken the day — they stop the obvious from arriving as ambush, and they convert background dread into present attention. Queen Mallikā's questions (MN 87) do the same work interpersonally. The people you will someday grieve are alive right now; anticipatory grief, faced, becomes the strongest argument ever made for the visit, the call, the recorded conversation, the repaired estrangement — while repairing still helps.

Finishing clean

DN 16 is the canon's manual for endings — jobs, eras, lives. The Buddha's last months: resolve open questions (he asks the assembly three times if anyone has doubts), release guilt in others (his final meal's donor is explicitly absolved), hand over what people need (the teaching, not a successor — the work outlives the worker by design), and let go on schedule. The checklist generalizes to every ending you'll face: questions answered, guilt released, handover complete, exit on time. Most estates settle the money and leave the rest — the questions never asked, the absolutions never given — unprobated forever. MN 131 holds the deadline honestly ("who knows, death may come tomorrow — there is no bargaining with mortality") and offers its mercy: the account settles daily; one fully-lived day is complete in itself.

Playbook

  • Grieving: find your mustard-seed walk — one room of people who've carried it too (Thig 10.1).
  • Accompanying: presence, one errand, and your own dead named plainly; explanations withheld (Thig 10.1, AN 5.198).
  • Daily recollections → weekly act: whom does today's "separation" line point at? Make the call (AN 5.57).
  • Run DN 16's checklist on your own affairs now: questions, guilt, handover, timing (DN 16).

10. Self, Identity & Growth

Finding yourself, imposter syndrome, moods, habit change, envy, and reinvention

"Finding your true self"

The search assumes a fixed core that the chariot argument dissolves: "you" names a working configuration — parts arranged and functioning — not a hidden essence awaiting discovery in Bali. The productive question swaps agonizing for maintenance: not "who am I really?" but "what configuration am I running, and is it roadworthy?" Parts can be repaired, re-arranged, upgraded; essences can only be searched for. This is why role-loss (job, marriage, sport, parenthood) triggers vertigo out of proportion to the logistics: what ended was a configuration mistaken for the self, and SN 22.59's control-test explains the mistake — none of the components ever obeyed like property, so none of them was ever the owner.

Imposter syndrome and the counterfeit self

Imposter feeling runs on the same identity-fixation Somā's demon used: SN 5.2 — the voice of "people like you can't" only lands on a mind that has agreed to be a "people like you," and her reply refuses the frame itself: the category is not where ability lives. The Six-Eared Macaque adds the stranger, deeper cut: the mind harbors a genuine counterfeit — performance indistinguishable from sincerity, rationalization wearing wisdom's face — and no external authority can spot yours for you. The honest resolution of imposter syndrome isn't reassurance ("you belong!") but discernment practice: competence is checkable against the work (AN 3.65's consequences-test), while the imposter feeling is a mood — and moods, per the next section, are not verdicts.

Moods that impersonate verdicts

SN 12.61 inverts the culture's deepest assumption: the mind is the least stable thing you have — "arising as one thing and ceasing as another all day and all night," a monkey grabbing branches — while the body at least persists for decades. The midnight despair-self, the dawn resolve-self, and the Friday who forgot both are branch-grabs, not judges. The practice is timestamping: "this is the 11 p.m. mind" filed as weather data, with big decisions deferred across a few branch-releases before any single monkey's grip gets to sign anything. MN 131 keeps the balance: presence isn't passivity — today's effort still gets made; it just doesn't get made by the mood.

Habit change and self-improvement

The canon's behavior-change stack is complete and sequenced. MN 19 is the law (repetition is destiny — every indulged loop is a vote for next year's mind) and the maturity curve (heavy steering early, light watching later). MN 20 is the toolkit — five escalating techniques, substitution first and willpower last, the exact reverse of how untrained people fight themselves into exhaustion. MN 117 is the loop architecture: know the wrong version specifically, catch it in flight, replace it — detection-and-substitution rather than vague resolve. MN 107 adds prerequisites (fix the floor below the wobbling one), and MN 36 stands guard against the whole project's failure mode: the inner drill sergeant, whose six-year randomized trial came back negative. Self-improvement that runs on self-attack is sawing the branch it sits on.

Envy, comparison, and reinvention

Comparison's perceptual error — your insides against their highlight reel — was covered under Digital Life; the deeper training is muditā (SN 46.54), which the tradition ranks the hardest of the four brahmavihāras precisely because envy is load-bearing in the untrained psyche. The rep: name a rival's success specifically and wish it larger — what it costs you to do that is the exact measurement of what envy was costing you continuously. For reinvention after failure or shame, AN 3.99 is the charter: the salt cannot be un-dropped; become the river — respond to the past not by re-litigating the act but by expanding the life around it until it stops flavoring everything. And Dhp 160/165 holds both edges of the ledger: no one else can corrupt or purify you — the weight and the freedom in one line.

Playbook

  • Identity check: list your top three "I am" statements; mark which are configurations wearing essence-costumes (Milinda).
  • Imposter spike: competence → check against work; feeling → timestamp as weather (SN 5.2, SN 12.61).
  • No decisions signed by the midnight monkey; 48-hour multi-mood review for anything big (SN 12.61).
  • Habit project: one target, MN 20's tools in order, drill sergeant fired (MN 20, MN 36).
  • One muditā rep per comparison spike (SN 46.54).

11. Decisions & Uncertainty

Choosing under fog, analysis paralysis, sunk costs, and settled questions

The triangulation standard

AN 3.65 is a complete decision epistemology. Its ten rejected criteria — tradition, lineage, hearsay, scripture, pure logic, inference, surface plausibility, agreement with existing views, speaker charisma, "he is our teacher" — are a catalog of how humans actually decide, including the modern variants (the thread, the influencer, the consensus of your feed). The approved method triangulates three checks: personal verification, consultation of the wise (not the loud — the wise, people whose lives you'd trade outcomes with), and observed consequences when acted upon. Note what this refuses on both flanks: credulity, and also the lazy skepticism that tests nothing. And the four assurances close the deal for decisions under irreducible uncertainty: choose the option that wins on every branch of the tree — a life of non-hatred pays off whatever the metaphysics turn out to be. Decision theory calls it a dominant strategy; the Kālāmas got it first.

Analysis paralysis and the arrow

MN 63 triages questions by a single criterion: would any answer change the treatment? The man refusing the surgeon until he knows the archer's caste and the fletching's feathers "would die with all that still unknown" — and the modern forms are everywhere: the career-changer who must first resolve their life's meaning, the couple that can't commit until every hypothetical is settled, the researcher of purchases who never purchases. The discipline: sort your open questions into fletching (fascinating, no answer changes your next action) and wound (an answer changes what you do Monday). Fund only the second category. MN 9 adds the ten-second root-check that outperforms an hour of justification: is this option growing from wanting, aversion, or fog — or from clarity? The rationalization layer lies; the root usually doesn't.

Sunk costs and the raft

The raft simile (MN 22) is the canon's sunk-cost teaching: the raft that genuinely carried you across — the degree, the strategy, the identity, the method — becomes weight the moment the crossing is done, and carrying it "on your head" out of gratitude is honoring the past by taxing the future. The test isn't whether it was valuable (it was; that's what makes sunk costs sticky) but whether it's load-bearing now. Tālapuṭa's poem (Thag 19.1) covers the other half of commitment math: the bargaining voice that re-litigates every chosen path the moment it's secured has a documented record of defecting on its own contracts — hold it to its record before renegotiating with it.

Settled means settled

A quiet gem from the Vinaya: Pācittiya 63 makes it an offense to knowingly reopen an issue that was properly settled — the early community learned that relitigating consumes more than the original dispute. The personal translation is a decision-hygiene rule: distinguish new information (reopens legitimately) from old feelings on a new night (doesn't), and give your settled decisions the procedural respect of staying settled between genuine updates. The 2 a.m. relitigation session (SN 12.61) almost never carries new information.

Playbook

  • Big choices: triangulate — test, consult the wise, watch consequences; charisma and consensus are not inputs (AN 3.65).
  • Under irreducible uncertainty: pick the every-branch winner (AN 3.65's four assurances).
  • Sort open questions: fletching vs. wound; defund the fletching (MN 63).
  • Ten-second root-check before committing (MN 9).
  • Reopen decisions for new information only — never for old feelings after midnight (Pc 63, SN 12.61).

12. Ethics in a Gray World

Complicity, spin, whistleblowing, and integrity as infrastructure

The five trades, updated

AN 5.177 names five businesses a lay follower shouldn't ply — weapons, living beings, meat, intoxicants, poison — and the underlying principle (profiting from harm at scale) reaches further than five industries. The honest modern questions: does my work profit from engineered compulsion (the intoxicants clause has opinions about attention-harvesting and loot boxes), from what functions as poison, from harm to living beings at scale? Few jobs are purely clean, and the sutta's realistic use isn't purity — it's a drift-compass: noticing when your role is migrating toward harm-for-profit and steering while steering is cheap. The Vinaya's economics rules (NP 18–20: no money handling, no trade, no speculation for monastics) mark the other pole — a life engineered to make greed structurally difficult — useful as a reference direction even for those of us holding jobs and mortgages.

Spin, framing, and the water dipper

MN 61 sets the standard at the foundation: the deliberate lie "throws away" one's spiritual worth like tipped-out water — not because lying is maximally wicked but because the shameless liar has discarded the instrumentation (honest self-report) that every other virtue depends on for maintenance. The gray zone is where modern life actually operates: the résumé's generous framing, the metric chosen because it flatters, the true statement arranged to produce a false impression. The canon's four speech categories (false, divisive, harsh, useless — MN 9) catch the last case: technically true speech engineered to mislead is divisive or useless speech wearing truth's badge. The operational test is the impression, not the sentence: what does the listener now believe, and is it so?

Unwelcome truth and its timing

Whistleblowing, the hard performance conversation, the family intervention — all governed by MN 58's stick-in-the-mouth clause: when someone you're responsible for (or to) is in danger, unwelcome truth is care and silence is the unkindness — and the timing gate still applies, because truth delivered for the speaker's relief at the listener's worst moment fails as help while succeeding as self-expression. The complicity question — "it's not my department" — meets MN 135's non-transferable ledger: participation is action, silence in the loop is action, and the ledger records what you did, not what the org chart said your department was.

Integrity as infrastructure, not ornament

The canon's deepest ethical claim is architectural: blamelessness outvalues wealth sixteen to one (AN 4.62) not as piety but as engineering — the fruits of practice (DN 2) begin with the lightness of having nothing to hide, and MN 7's sixteen stains list contempt, envy, and stinginess alongside hatred because the socially tolerated vices corrode the same infrastructure. Ethics, in this frame, is what makes the rest of the mind's work possible: attention built over a guilty life won't stabilize (MN 107's ordering), and the mirror practice (MN 61 — before, during, after) plus confession-and-correction (the Vinaya model) is the complete maintenance cycle: check, act, review, disclose, repair, proceed. No shame spiral is part of the procedure — the drill sergeant was fired in MN 36.

Playbook

  • Annual livelihood drift-check against the harm-at-scale principle (AN 5.177).
  • Spin test: judge the impression created, not the sentence's technical truth (MN 61, MN 9).
  • Unwelcome truths: duty confirmed (whose stick, whose mouth?), then timing chosen for reception (MN 58).
  • Weekly mirror review: one harm found → disclose, correct, close — no spiral (MN 61).
  • Fund the blamelessness account before the brokerage account (AN 4.62).

13. Success, Status & Fame

Ambition's ceiling, praise addiction, platforms, and what outlasts applause

Havoc in heaven

The definitive portrait of status-craving is the Monkey King's pre-pilgrimage career: immortality stolen, heaven's armies defeated, the title "Great Sage Equal to Heaven" demanded and granted — and still not enough, because the ego's hunger is structural: each acquisition resets the baseline rather than closing the gap. The canonical analysis is craving-for-becoming (SN 56.11's second channel): the drive isn't for the thing but for being the one who has it, and identities, unlike appetites, have no satiation mechanism. This is why the summit disappoints reliably enough to be a cliché among those who reach it: the wager was that enough recognition finally equals peace, and the ignoble search predicts the result before the climb starts.

Praise addiction and the platform treadmill

Praise is the sweetest of the eight winds and pharmacologically the most habit-forming: metrics made it dosable, and an audience-holding life becomes a dependency on the wind blowing one direction — which it structurally will not. DN 1's opening training (elation at praise clouds judgment as surely as deflation at blame) is the influencer's missing manual, and the Aṭṭhakavagga describes the elation-dejection cycle keyed to audience outcomes as the debater's wound — both wounds. The stabilizer is work-referenced rather than reception-referenced identity: the quality of the thing made is checkable (AN 3.65's consequences), while its reception is weather belonging to the eight-wind system, not to you.

The quiet contributors

The canon consistently honors the unglamorous factors: Sha Wujing carrying luggage through all hundred chapters, the dragon-horse walking every mile under the mission, and — canonically — the acrobat's apprentice, whose self-stabilizing is the whole act's safety, and Khujjuttarā (Iti 27's transmitter), an enslaved servant whose memory carried a canonical book. Status economies systematically misprice this work — maintenance, care, reliability, institutional memory — and the Maṅgala Sutta's blessing-ladder reprices it: supporting family, blameless work, and steadfastness rank as highest blessings while visibility appears nowhere on the list. A career audit against the maṅgala list instead of the org chart reads very differently.

Legacy

DN 16 is the founder's own legacy design, and it is pointed: no successor, no relic-cult encouraged for monastics, authority handed to the teaching and the procedure — the work engineered to outlive the worker as work, not as monument. Paired with MN 135's portable ledger (what follows you is what you did) and AN 5.57's fifth recollection, the canon's answer to the legacy question is almost deflationarily practical: build things that run without your name on them, train people who don't need you, and let the applause fall where it falls — it was never in your control column anyway (SN 22.59).

Playbook

  • Summit check: is this drive for the work or for being The One Who — and what's the evidence the second kind ever satiates? (SN 56.11)
  • Reception detox: one shipped piece of work per month whose metrics you don't check (AN 8.6).
  • Audit the year against the maṅgala ladder, not the org chart (Khp 5).
  • Legacy design: what runs without your name on it? Build that (DN 16).

14. Universal Truths

What has survived every century that tested it

Strip the cosmology, the robes, and the Iron-Age furniture, and a short list of claims has held from the Ganges basin to the feed in your pocket — each one checkable against your own data this week:

  • Everything conditional is temporary, and suffering comes from contracting around what changes as if it were supposed to hold still (SN 56.11).
  • Pain is mandatory; suffering is manufactured — the second arrow is always optional with training (SN 36.6).
  • What you rehearse, you become — repetition is destiny, in thought as in action (MN 19).
  • Hatred never ends by hatred — retaliation is a perpetual-motion machine with no off-switch on its own side (Dhp 5).
  • You become your company — including the company that doesn't know you exist (SN 45.2).
  • Everyone is the center of their own world — and knowing it is the foundation of real ethics (SN 3.8).
  • No one else can corrupt or purify you — the ledger is non-transferable (AN 5.57, MN 135).
  • The winds rotate — gain, loss, praise, blame visit everyone; maturity is non-capsizing, not exemption (AN 8.6).
  • Craving's promise is structurally false — acquisition resets the baseline instead of closing the gap, in careers, purchases, and titles alike (MN 26).
  • Your own elaborations attack you — the story built from the sigh, not the sigh, is what you're fighting by minute three (MN 18).
  • Effort has an optimum, not a maximum — and overexertion snaps to quitting, not to sustainability (AN 6.55).
  • Honest people deadlock on partial data — everyone is touching real elephant; certainty of completeness is the error (Ud 6.4).
  • The past is dilutable — not erasable, but a growing life turns the same salt from ruinous to unnoticeable (AN 3.99).
  • Moods are not verdicts — the mind changes faster than the body; never let the midnight monkey sign anything (SN 12.61).
  • Discipline internalized stops feeling like restraint — the headband, fully outgrown, is found to be already gone (Journey West; MN 44).

If the four-stage curriculum is the course, this list is what should still be in your pocket ten years after taking it.
