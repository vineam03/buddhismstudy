These texts were composed in a world of kings, castes, and rice paddies — but they were composed about craving, status, love, anger, and death, which have not shipped a new version since. Each lens below takes one region of contemporary life and shows where the canon already mapped it. Every claim links to its source card in the library.

Career & Work

Ambition, burnout, performance reviews, and what a job is for

The promotion treadmill is the canon's opening case study: acquire, plateau, recalibrate, crave again — MN 26 calls chasing durable security through perishable positions "the ignoble search," and predicts the anxiety attached to it is structural, unfixable by the next title. Performance feedback is the eight worldly winds with a dashboard: praise/blame and gain/loss now arrive as metrics, but they rotate exactly as scheduled, and careers capsize not from the winds but from treating good quarters as identity and bad ones as verdict.

Burnout was diagnosed by a lute: AN 6.55 — effort has an optimum, not a maximum, and overtightened strings don't step down gracefully, they snap to quitting. The grind-culture corrective is even stronger in MN 36: the founder personally ran the maximum-intensity experiment for six years and reported it a failure. Skill-building follows MN 107's apprenticeship model — staged competencies, prerequisites, no skipped floors — and what you may do for money has a floor: AN 5.177's banned trades share one principle, profiting from harm at scale, which is a live question in more industries than five.

The toxic team is Kosambī (MN 48): groups fracture when cordiality-maintenance stops, and the repair order is counterintuitive — restore small acts of goodwill first, argue the issue second. And work as identity gets the chariot treatment (Milinda): a job title names a configuration, not an essence — which is why layoffs feel like death to those who thought the title was the self, and like reconfiguration to those who didn't.

Love & Relationships

Attachment, marriage, friendship, and the price on the tag

The contract nobody reads: MN 87 states it plainly — sorrow is born from the beloved; loving mortal beings is exposure to grief, no negotiation available. This is not an argument against love; it's the honest price tag, and Queen Mallikā's questions ("do you love her? would her change grieve you?") convert the dread into present-tense appreciation. Real partnership is bilateral infrastructure: DN 31 assigns duties in both directions — respect, fidelity, delegation, generosity — a maintenance schedule, not a feeling.

The fight you keep having runs on papañca (MN 18): a two-second tone of voice becomes, three minutes of proliferation later, evidence about the entire relationship — and MN 21's maid-test says your character in the relationship is measured only under provocation; everything else is favorable weather. Repair follows MN 48 (goodwill acts before issue litigation) and AN 5.161's resentment ladder, whose fifth rung — "their conduct is their own ledger" — releases the exhausting volunteer-judge job.

Love vs. attachment: the canon's mettā (Snp 1.8) is goodwill — wanting their welfare; its near-enemy is affection that loves people for what they provide, which is craving wearing love's clothes. The test: how do you respond when they change, need less of you, or choose something you wouldn't? Supporting a struggling partner without drowning is the acrobats' rule (SN 47.19): your own stability is the load-bearing contribution. And who you become together is SN 45.2: companionship isn't half the path — it's the whole thing; partners are co-trainers whether or not they mean to be.

Money & Security

Earning, spending, debt, and the account that actually compounds

The canon is not anti-money — it gave its personal-finance teachings to a banker (AN 4.62) and endorsed earning and enjoying. But it ranks the accounts: ownership, enjoyment, and debtlessness together are worth a sixteenth of blamelessness — no portfolio compensates for things to hide. Debt gets the sharpest line in ancient literature: the debtless man is unafraid — debt is fear with a payment schedule.

Budgeting is in DN 31 (enjoy a quarter, invest half, save a quarter) and AN 8.54's reservoir: most people optimize the inlet (income) while ignoring the open channels — the four classic drains (intoxication, gambling, affairs, bad company) plus their modern equivalents on subscription. Retail therapy is the second arrow monetized: numbing purchased because no other pain-exit was ever taught. And the fear under it all — that enough money will finally equal safety — is the ignoble search again (MN 26): perishable stores can be enjoyed but not leaned on; the leaning is what generates the anxiety that more acquisition claims to cure.

Conflict & Communication

Arguments, feedback, resentment, and the internet

Before the hard conversation: MN 58's three gates — true, beneficial, timely — with "welcome" deliberately not a gate: some truths must wound (the stick in the child's mouth), and many true things fail the useful-or-timely gates and should die unsaid. AN 5.198 adds tone and inner state: the same sentence from goodwill and from irritation are different communications, and everyone can tell.

The comment section was described in the canon's oldest poems: the Aṭṭhakavagga's debate culture — elation when your position wins, dejection when it loses, both wounds — is the quote-tweet economy with older furniture. The elephant explains the deadlock between honest people: everyone is touching real elephant; everyone mistakes their patch for the animal. Anger itself is costed in AN 7.64 — sleep, judgment, wealth, alliances, face: seven payments from your account, delivered to your adversary free. And grudges have the five-rung ladder, built for people who — like ancient villagers — cannot actually block or move away from their resenter.

Loss, Aging & Mortality

Grief, the aging body, and deadlines that are real

Grief gets the canon's gentlest engineering: Kisāgotamī (Thig 10.1) is not argued out of her loss — she's given an errand whose completion is the understanding: no house is without its dead. The movement from "why me" to "so this is what everyone has been carrying" is the door, and it opens through contact with other grievers (the mustard-seed walk, institutionalized today as the grief group). SN 15.3 supplies the scale: your loss belongs to the oldest current in sentient life.

Aging is handled without cosmetics: the Buddha at eighty describes his own body as an old cart held together with straps (DN 16) and works until the last afternoon. The five recollections are the anti-ambush practice — ninety seconds a day of the facts, so aging arrives as schedule rather than scandal. And the deadline: MN 131 — "today the effort must be made; who knows, death may come tomorrow" — with its merciful accounting: the day settles daily; one fully-lived day is complete in itself.

Self & Identity

Imposter syndrome, reinvention, moods, and 'finding yourself'

"Finding your true self" assumes a fixed core the chariot argument dissolves: 'you' names a working configuration, not a hidden essence — so the productive question isn't "who am I really?" but "what configuration am I running, and is it roadworthy?" Parts can be repaired; essences can only be agonized over. Identity crisis after a role ends (job, marriage, sport, parenting) is SN 22.59 in the wild: the components were never the owner, which is why their loss can't actually delete you — it deletes a costume.

Moods that impersonate verdicts: SN 12.61 — the mind changes faster than the body; the midnight despair-self and the dawn resolve-self are branch-grabs, not judges. Timestamp them. Stereotype and self-doubt got their permanent answer from a nun: Somā's reply to "people like you can't" is not "I can despite being X" (which accepts the frame) but "the category is not where ability lives." And the inner critic running a punishment regime has founder-level counter-evidence: six years of world-class self-attack bought nothing; the breakthrough question was "why am I afraid of wholesome pleasure?"

Universal Truths

What has survived every century that tested it

Strip the cosmology, the robes, and the Iron-Age furniture, and a short list of claims has held from the Ganges basin to the feed in your pocket:

  • Everything conditional is temporary, and suffering comes from contracting around what changes as if it were supposed to hold still (SN 56.11).
  • Pain is mandatory; suffering is manufactured — the second arrow is always optional with training (SN 36.6).
  • What you rehearse, you become — repetition is destiny, in thought as in action (MN 19).
  • Hatred never ends by hatred — retaliation is a perpetual-motion machine with no off-switch on its own side (Dhp 5).
  • You become your company (SN 45.2).
  • Everyone is the center of their own world — and knowing it is the foundation of real ethics (SN 3.8).
  • No one else can corrupt or purify you — the ledger is non-transferable (AN 5.57, MN 135).
  • The winds rotate — gain, loss, praise, blame visit everyone; maturity is non-capsizing, not exemption (AN 8.6).

If the four-stage curriculum is the course, this list is what should still be in your pocket ten years after taking it.
