How this works. Each unit builds on the last — the order follows the four-stage curriculum: first see the problem clearly, then understand its machinery, then train, then turn outward. Check off readings as you go (progress is saved in your browser). Suggested pace: one unit per week or two, reading each text's card in the library and doing the unit's practice before moving on. Don't rush units 1–2; everything later stands on them.

Unit 1 — First Contact: the shape of the problem

Why first: the entire teaching is a response to one diagnosis. Until the diagnosis feels personally true — not gloomy, just accurate — the rest reads as philosophy.

Read, in order

  • SN 56.11 — The first sermon: the whole system in one page
  • SN 36.6 — The two arrows: pain vs. suffering
  • AN 8.6 — The eight worldly winds: the weather system of a life
  • AN 5.57 — The five daily recollections: the facts under the denial
  • Dhammapada — essential verses: the pocket version

Practice this unit

Two-arrow journaling: once a day, take one unpleasant moment and write down what was the first arrow and what you added. Say the five recollections each morning — ninety seconds.

Unit 2 — Impermanence, loss, and mortality

Why now: Unit 1 named the mechanism; this unit takes it to full scale — love, grief, death — before the analysis gets technical. The teaching earns trust here or nowhere.

Read, in order

  • MN 87 — Born from the beloved: what love costs, said out loud
  • Thig 10.1 — Kisāgotamī and the mustard seed: grief's actual cure
  • SN 15.3 — The tears: your loss in species-scale perspective
  • DN 16 — The Buddha's last days: the founder exempts himself from nothing
  • MN 131 — One fine night: urgency without panic

Practice this unit

Run Queen Mallikā's questions (from MN 87's frame) on your own beloveds — and let the answer convert into one act of present appreciation per day rather than dread.

Unit 3 — The engine room: how suffering assembles

Why now: with the problem felt, open the hood. This is Stage 2's machinery — craving, the causal chain, and the runaway story-factory.

Read, in order

  • MN 9 — Right view: acts, roots, and the map of wholesome/unwholesome
  • SN 12.2 — Dependent origination analyzed: the twelve-link supply chain
  • MN 18 — The honey-ball: papañca, the proliferation cascade
  • SN 12.61 — The monkey mind: why the mind is the worst place to look for a self
  • DN 15 — The great causes: the chain scaled up to social conflict

Practice this unit

Reverse-engineer one bad evening through the links: contact → feeling → craving → clinging → the fight. Find your personal handoff point — the moment feeling became craving.

Unit 4 — Not-self: the owner who isn't there

Why now: not-self without Units 1–3 sounds like nihilism; after them it's just the chain's last link examined. The hardest idea, placed where it can land.

Read, in order

  • SN 22.59 — The not-self characteristic: the control argument
  • Mil — Milinda's chariot: person as configuration, not essence
  • Ud 1.10 — Bāhiya: perception without a proprietor
  • SN 5.2 — Somā's answer: identity-fixation is the hook doubt uses
  • SN 35.28 — The fire sermon: the senses as combustion

Practice this unit

Language experiment for one week: "anger is present" instead of "I am angry"; "the 11 p.m. mind" instead of "the truth." Watch what changes when states stop being verdicts about a self.

Unit 5 — Views, opinions, and honest thinking

Why now: armed with not-self, you can examine the most defended possession of all — your opinions — without it feeling like an attack.

Read, in order

  • AN 3.65 — The Kālāmas: how to vet any claim, including these
  • Ud 6.4 — The blind men and the elephant: honest data, partial contact
  • Snp 4 — The Aṭṭhakavagga: the oldest voice, on view-clinging
  • MN 22 — The water-snake and the raft: even truth, wrongly held, injures
  • MN 63 — The poisoned arrow: triaging questions by usefulness
  • DN 1 — The net of views: the full 62-position field guide

Practice this unit

One week without entering a single opinion-tournament (online or in person) while still holding your views. Journal the withdrawal symptoms — that's the clinging, measured.

Unit 6 — Ethics as foundation: speech, work, money

Why now: Stage 3 begins. Ethics comes before meditation in every canonical sequence — a guilty, leaky life generates the noise that practice then fails against.

Read, in order

  • MN 61 — The water dipper: truthfulness as the load-bearing wall
  • MN 58 — To Prince Abhaya: the true/useful/timely gates
  • AN 5.198 — Well-spoken speech: the five marks
  • DN 31 — Advice to Sigāla: the six directions, friends, and the budget
  • AN 4.62 — Four happinesses: debtlessness and blamelessness
  • AN 8.54 — Lay prosperity: the reservoir and its four drains
  • AN 5.177 — The five trades: livelihood boundaries
  • MN 135 — Action's results: character as compounding cause
  • AN 3.99 — The lump of salt: the past is dilutable
  • Browse the 227 Vinaya rules: ethics as institution — read one category's origin stories

Practice this unit

Pick one: a week of the three speech gates before every hard message, or the DN 31 six-direction audit with one concrete repair per direction.

Unit 7 — Training the mind: effort, thought, breath

Why now: with the ethical floor laid, formal mind-training has something to stand on. This unit is the practice core of the whole sequence.

Read, in order

  • MN 107 — Gradual training: the curriculum with prerequisites
  • AN 6.55 — The lute strings: calibrating effort
  • MN 19 — Two kinds of thought: repetition is destiny
  • MN 20 — Removing distracting thoughts: the five escalating tools
  • MN 2 — All the taints: seven tools, matched to problems
  • MN 118 — Breath meditation: the sixteen steps (start with four)
  • MN 10 — Satipaṭṭhāna and DN 22: the four fields
  • MN 36 — The austerities: why self-punishment fails
  • MN 7 — The cloth: the sixteen stains audit
  • MN 121 — Emptiness: subtractive attention, floor by floor

Practice this unit

Establish the daily sit: ten minutes of MN 118's first tetrad, lute-string checked (too tight? too loose?). Add one MN 20 technique for your dominant intrusive-thought pattern.

Unit 8 — Turning outward: the relational training

Why now: Stage 4. Warmth practiced before stability tends to be performance; after Units 6–7 it can be real. This is where the training faces other people.

Read, in order

  • Snp 1.8 — The Metta Sutta: the core text and its prerequisites
  • AN 11.15 — Mettā's eleven benefits: why it's worth training
  • SN 46.54 — The brahmavihāras developed: feeling vs. trained capacity
  • MN 21 — The saw: the ceiling that deletes excuses
  • AN 7.64 — Anger's seven gifts to your enemies
  • AN 5.161 — Removing resentment: the five-rung ladder
  • MN 48 — Kosambī: repairing a fractured group
  • SN 47.19 — The acrobats: boundaries that protect everyone
  • SN 3.8 — Dearer than self: honest empathy's foundation
  • MN 62 — The elements: meditating like earth under insult
  • Iti 27 — Goodwill outshines them all
  • SN 45.2 — Friendship is the whole of it

Practice this unit

Daily five-minute mettā with a widening circle — including, by week's end, your current most difficult person. Apply the AN 5.161 ladder to one standing resentment, taking whichever rung holds.

Unit 9 — Integration: the long view

Why last: these texts assume everything before them — the autobiography, the full path analyses, the poetry of people far along, and the wider map of the whole canon.

Read, in order

  • MN 26 — The noble search: the two searches, and the founder's own arc
  • DN 2 — The fruits of practice: the whole path as visible results
  • MN 141 — The truths analyzed and MN 117 — The great forty: the reference architecture
  • MN 44 — Dhammadinnā's answers: the trainings integrated
  • Thag 19.1 — Tālapuṭa and Snp 1.3 — The rhinoceros horn: the long game's inner weather
  • Khp 5 — The highest blessings: the whole life, as a ladder
  • DN 26 — The wheel-turning monarch: the teaching at societal scale
  • Read the Canon cover-to-cover guide: place everything you've read on the full map
  • Capstones: Modern Life and Journey to the West — the teaching translated into your world, and into story

Practice this unit

Write your own one-page 'first sermon': the diagnosis, mechanism, and daily method as you now understand them, in your own words, for a friend who's never read any of this. That document is the real exam.

After Unit 9: loop back. The units re-read differently after the first pass — Unit 2 in particular. And the full-site PDF export makes the whole course printable for offline study.
